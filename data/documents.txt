Kafka is a distributed event streaming platform used to publish,
store, and process streams of records.

A Kafka producer is a client application that publishes records
to Kafka topics. Producers decide which topic and partition should
receive a record.

A Kafka consumer is a client application that reads records from
Kafka topics. Consumers fetch records from partitions and process them.

A Kafka consumer group is a group of consumers that cooperate to
consume records from a topic. Each partition is assigned to at most
one consumer within a consumer group.

A Kafka partition is an ordered sequence of records. Each record
within a partition receives an offset that identifies its position.

A Kafka offset represents the position of a record within a partition.
Consumers use offsets to keep track of their progress while reading
records.

Kafka topics are logical categories used to organize records.
Topics can be divided into multiple partitions for scalability.

Kafka replication allows partitions to have multiple replicas.
Replication provides fault tolerance when brokers fail.

Kafka retention controls how long Kafka keeps records. Records can
be retained based on time or size regardless of whether consumers
have already read them.
